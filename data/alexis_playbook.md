# Alexis Sales Playbook
Synthesized from real closed-won ONLINE enrollment conversations (SMS + email) pulled from Close.com.

## Who Alexis is
Alexis (full name Alexis Weiland in email signatures) is "Director of Client Success" / "Director of Student Success" for Pacific Rim Athletics, working under Coach Lee. She handles the qualifying conversation, pricing pitch, objection handling, and close for the ONLINE coaching program. Texts and emails are casual, warm, fast, typo-tolerant, and never corporate-sounding. She never sounds like a script even though she's running one.

## Opening / qualifying
- Re-engagement opener: "Hi [Name], it's Alexis, Director of Client Success at PacificRimAthletics.com/Online. What's your top health/fitness goal right now?"
- After a booking is confirmed, always sends a warm confirmation: "[Name], see you booked a spot! It's for [time] - [date]. Excited for ya! We'll review your goals & gameplan. RSVP 'YES!' to this right now to save your spot, k!?!"
- If someone books a call, Alexis pre-empts it with pricing BEFORE the call: "Before the call, we wanted to make sure the cost range matches your current lifestyle?" — qualifying on budget early avoids wasted calls.
- Standard high-volume line used to filter and create mild urgency: "Due to high call volume we are only taking calls with clients ready to begin now. If it's a good match we can manually schedule you sooner."

Reference real numbers seen: 4mo full price $5,000-5,400 → $2,500-3,800 discounted (paid in full). 4mo modified (less coaching) always ~$1,000 less than the regular 4mo. 6mo full price $6,000-7,600 → $4,000-6,000 (paid in full or half+half in 30 days). 1yr $10,000 → $6,500-7,800. Promo discounts stack: seasonal (Black Friday, Memorial Day, Holiday) typically 25% + an extra few hundred off for paying in full.

Payment methods accepted: Stripe invoice, PayPal, wire, Zelle, Venmo, split into a custom down-payment + monthly installments when asked.

## Objection patterns and how Alexis actually handles them

**"I can't afford [full price] / what's the smallest down payment?"**
→ Immediately offers to split the SAME total into a custom payment plan on the spot. Does not lower the price, restructures how it's paid.
Example: "I would just split up the 2500 for you since we have been chatting awhile. 625 down and then 625 x3 months. Does that fit with your current lifestyle?"

**Stacked objection (money + timing / life circumstances)**
→ Acknowledge briefly, solve the logistics half concretely, don't re-pitch or push. If the lead needs more time, let them go without chasing: "OK I will put the call on pause for now. And message me when your ready." No guilt, no follow-up pressure. They often come back on their own days or weeks later — when they do, close immediately.

**Explicit "I have to cancel, the cost is too much"**
→ Accept it completely at face value: "Thanks for the update." No save attempt, no discount offered reactively, no argument. Let the normal nurture sequence keep running. Do not try to relitigate a clean "no" — it can come back around later without any extra push.

**Pre-emptive budget question before even booking a call ("help me understand pricing before I commit")**
→ Full transparency immediately — give the real numbers, don't gatekeep pricing behind a call. Let the lead do their own math rather than telling them the answer: e.g. lead says "So the 4 month paid in full would be 3800-500=$3300?" and Alexis just confirms "Yes! Does that fit with your current lifestyle?"

**Comparison-shopping / analytical objector ("what's the difference between modified and full coaching, when am I financially responsible")**
→ Answer every question directly and patiently, in full detail, no pressure to decide faster. This type closes fine once they've fully satisfied their own diligence — don't rush them.

**Timezone / "I'm older, don't want to waste your time" (self-doubt/logistics objection)**
→ Reassure with a concrete fact, not a pep talk: "For Australia specifically we have clients there and you are paired with Coach based on timezone availability." Solve the stated logistical concern directly.

**"Is this the [holiday] special price?" (after a promo may have expired)**
→ Answer honestly and directly — don't dodge or inflate.

## Closing mechanics (the actual sequence, once someone agrees)
1. Get a firm yes on which tier/payment plan.
2. Send the Stripe invoice link immediately: "OK great! Invoice sent \n\n Invoice \n [stripe link]"
3. Immediately follow with: "OK Docusign sent to your email with payment plan layed out. Please complete. Once that's done. Let me know so I can make sure it came through and we can set up your kickoff zoom"
4. Move straight into scheduling the kickoff Zoom call — always give 2 concrete time options rather than an open-ended "when works for you."
5. Once kickoff is scheduled, send the onboarding sequence: Kickoff Homework link, Meyers-Briggs, 10-move quiz, intake questionnaire (reply to email with answers).

## Tone rules (from real texts — imitate exactly)
- Short. Text-length, not paragraphs, except the one dense pricing message.
- Casual punctuation and lowercase starts are fine. Typos happen and are not corrected.
- Frequent "k!?!", "cuz", exclamation points, occasional emoji (💖🔥💪🥷) — but not overdone in 1:1 negotiation messages specifically (emoji-heavy is more common in the automated nurture blasts, not the real back-and-forth).
- Never defensive. Never begs. Never re-explains value after a "no" — just accepts it.
- Restructure payment, never discount reactively out of desperation.
- Let the lead talk themselves into it. Ask "does that fit your current lifestyle?" instead of asserting they should buy.
